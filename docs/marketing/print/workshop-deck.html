Not every job post is a job

  • Some posts stay up long after the job is filled, or were never real: ghost jobs
  • Reposts on job sites can be weeks older than they look
  • Check: is it still on the employer's own careers page?
  • Check: how long has it been up? Over 30 days is a yellow flag

Scam warning signs

  • Asks you to pay for training, equipment or a background check
  • Interview only by text or chat app, offer comes fast
  • Asks for your bank details or ID number before an offer
  • Company in the post doesn't match the site you apply on
  • Pay that is far above the going rate for the work

How screening works

  • Many employers sort resumes by how well they match the job's words
  • A recruiter often spends seconds on the first read
  • The fix is not more keywords. It is the job's words for things you really did
rework
6

Find the job's words

  • Underline the skills and tools the post repeats
  • Mark the ones you have done, even under another name
  • "Handled customer complaints" may be the job's "client escalations"
  • Missing a skill? Leave it out, or say how you are learning it. Never invent it
rework
7

Tailor in 4 moves

  • Lead with the title and summary this job wants, true to your experience
  • Move your most relevant bullets to the top
  • Use their words where they mean the same thing you did
  • Keep numbers real: if you don't know the number, don't make one up
